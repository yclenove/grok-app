**Detach residual, quarantine, and retained evidence**

Detach-by-M is a material improvement over detach-by-`/dev/diskN`: unrelated disk-number reuse does not make another disk occupy the private M pathname. For a non-interfered private namespace, fresh image/path/dev/inode/mount mapping plus M-bound argv is an acceptable resolution of round-1 finding 1 for this lab slice. It is not an attachment capability immune to namespace mutation. A same-user process can detach the owned image and mount another volume at M between verification and effect; pathname detach can then target that replacement. The runbook explicitly states at `:134` that simultaneous changes by other processes of the same user are outside this protection, and manual recovery at `:148` also uses the receipt's M. Do not claim a measured atomic identity binding or protection against same-UID interference. I am not reimposing the old global disk/hotplug exclusion after the controller chose this narrower M-bound boundary. Finding 2 concerns an additional principal admitted by unchecked ACLs, not that accepted residual.

Once `VOL_PHASE=quarantine` or `VOL_MAYBE=1` is set, `volume_down` issues no new detach/unlink/rmdir (`:1886`); `can_advance` blocks later real subcases (`:2418`). A skipped row resets in-memory volume fields but does not overwrite the retained receipt. Finalization removes scratch only; it does not delete `--out/volume-receipt`. VM containment teardown is still allowed through `cleanup_owned`; quarantine is not a promise that every resource operation in the script ceases. Fresh absence verification precedes unlink after successful detach and after failed attach. A replaced M fails its removal check and is retained, although the owned image can already have been unlinked before that mismatch is discovered (`:1770`, `:1771`).

There are two qualifications to the cleanup claim. First, ordinary measurement failures are not always latched as volume uncertainty: a timeout on the second/final `read_c9` is collapsed into `unknown/probe-failed` by `judge_c9`, unlike the first read's explicit quarantine branch (`:2021` versus `:2066`). An in-memory check returned `phase=mounted maybe=0 quarantine=0` for that timeout. An unconfirmed direct child still sets sticky HALT and blocks detach, but this is not uniform timeout-to-quarantine behavior. Second, `volio` is a shell that launches `stat`, `rm`, `rmdir`, and `mkdir`; `run_bounded` explicitly tracks only its direct child (`:167`). Killing/reaping that shell does not prove a blocked external descendant exited. A pending removal may complete after quarantine. Preserve the receipt and do not describe shell reaping as proof that the entire worker has stopped; use a single-process IO helper or explicitly supervise its IO descendants when completing the bounded-worker fix.

The receipt now retains original image and underlying mount/attempt identities and a lifecycle phase outside disposable scratch. This closes the earlier evidence-loss bug in ordinary quarantine paths. It is a single latest-attempt receipt, not a durable multi-attempt ledger; stopping later attempts after quarantine makes that sufficient for this slice. Receipt updates after mount/detach and quarantine are best-effort (`:1871`, `:1917`, `:1399`), so recovery must revalidate current state rather than assume the stored phase is current. The ACL/staging-file issue in finding 2 must be fixed before relying on the claimed protection of that receipt.

Retain the complete inventory walk, fresh normal/force verification, at-most-once force, stopped-VM requirement, identity comparisons, external receipt, observed peaks, and strict terminal framing while addressing them.
