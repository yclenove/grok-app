**F1 — P1: the attempt-wide byte cap terminates conforming long attempts**

Evidence: `tart/schedule.rs:32` defines `POLL_BYTE_BUDGET` as cumulative for an attempt. `tart/mailbox_guest.rs:35`, `:61`, and `:114` maintain a counter that never resets between calls. `finish_poll` charges successful frames, repeated/stale frames, and discarded lease ACKs (`:184`, `:197`) to that counter and permanently fails at 128 KiB. The test hook at `:339` explicitly acknowledges that the scheduled inventory traffic cannot fit.

For scale, a compact legal empty inventory envelope with `run1`, `boot1`, `desk1`, and `cfg1` is 464 bytes including framing. Only 282 such reads fit in 128 KiB; 1951 require at least 905,264 bytes using that representative size, before lease ACKs, larger sequence/sample numbers, side results, or stale-file rereads. A conforming full-length attempt using the claimed schedule therefore fails closed substantially before its task budget. Repeated visibility-delay polls make this worse. This is an availability defect in the proposed adapter, not evidence of a successful 2048-request schedule.

Minimal fix: scope the 128 KiB accounting to one bounded read/poll call and its internal retries. Retain retry evidence and the separate per-attempt 2048 request limits. Remove the production attempt-wide rejection and test-hook workaround. A frame-size bound of 4097 bytes already bounds the current single-read path (`tart/mailbox/slots.rs:640`); if a call performs multiple reads, charge all of them to that call's budget.

Regression: complete a full scheduled attempt through normal adapter IO, including stale reads, without clearing private in-flight state; separately exhaust the byte allowance within one read call and require failure closed.

**F2 — P1: ACK processing invents a new host deadline and can revive an expired grant**

Evidence: `tart/machine/io.rs:266` records the start of the current **lease poll**. After accepting an ACK, `:282` computes `grant_end = started + 2000`, then stores it at `:298`. It does not derive a conservative host deadline from the referenced guest sample or the originally published grant. `lease_matches` (`tart/machine/service.rs:533`) checks echoed numbers and checked `g+2000`, but no sample age, elapsed grant age, or previous-deadline continuity. Worse, `preamble` simply clears an expired `grant_until` (`:277`) and then permits renewal (`:284`).

A sequence of valid ACKs for the same inventory sample `g=0`, with `until_ms=2000`, can set host deadlines 2000, 2800, 3600, 4400 at polls beginning at 0, 800, 1600, 2400 ms. The guest deadline never moved. The supplied long traces actually return this constant sample/deadline (`s11b/m4_guests.rs:234`, `:241`). A delayed first ACK or an ACK after an earlier deadline can likewise be accepted against the start of the latest poll. The host can continue dispatching/adopting evidence after the guest must have stopped, contrary to the irreversible-expiry rule.

Minimal fix: retain each sent grant's referenced sample and conservative time bounds at publication; compute the host validity bound from that sample with checked arithmetic and the visibility allowance. Reusing a sample must not move the deadline. Check the old deadline before and after IO and permanently stop ordinary dispatch on expiry. A late ACK may be validated/discarded but must never establish new validity. Keep guest-clock and host-clock values distinct; they have no assumed common epoch.

Regression: replay the same sample across increasing lease sequences, delay a current ACK past its actual deadline, and deliver a fresh ACK after an expired older grant. None may revive work or extend the stored deadline. Also advance the sample while an ACK is pending: validation must use that ACK's saved grant, rather than the latest `track.sample_ms` as at `:544`.

**F3 — P1: the claimed 800 ms fresh-sample/grant schedule is not implemented**

Evidence: `tart/schedule.rs:75` computes counts from constants and assigns `age_ms=1000` and `gap_ms=800`; it does not establish those properties of machine IO. `should_renew` (`tart/machine/service.rs:382`) schedules from `lease_at`, which is written **when an ACK is read** (`tart/machine/io.rs:297`). The adapter keeps a lease open until its terminal is read and will not publish a newer grant meanwhile (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:177`, `:198`, `:398`). With a 1 s ACK round trip, the next publication is about 1 s + 800 ms after the preceding publication, not 800 ms. This consumes the alleged 200 ms slack and can miss the previous `g+2000` deadline.

Fresh inventory is also not a service scheduled by the renewal preamble. During isolation, after binding, calls only advance probes (`tart/machine/service.rs:147`); a probe group lasting several seconds repeatedly leases the initial sample. Inventory during long login/run is driven by completion polling (`:508`), not a scheduler that coordinates samples and grants. In the same step renewal runs before that completion inventory. A new inventory cannot be published until its prior terminal has been read (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:177`), so nonzero visibility/round-trip time also constrains the sample cadence.

The total-window arithmetic itself is correct and conservative: prep 600 s, including login; task 900 s; cleanup 60 s; 1,560,000/800 + 1 + 8 = 1959, leaving 89 requests. What is missing is a feasible implementation of that grid and a bound on sample-to-accept age through guest→host visibility, service delays, and host→guest visibility. The measured approximately 1 s round trip does not establish the hardcoded age/gap combination. A schedule that cannot satisfy both bounds must fail its gate.

Minimal fix: implement explicit fresh-inventory and grant publication scheduling across every boot-bound pre-revoke phase. Anchor cadence to publications/samples, independently of ACK arrival; support the ADR's lease overwrite/stale-ACK rule. Include actual pending/visibility and service-delay bounds in feasibility, and reject infeasible conditions instead of returning a nominal proof.

Regression: drive `Attempt` plus `MailboxGuestChannel` plus a clock-driven guest with asymmetric visibility, 1 s round trips, ENOENT, skipped lease versions, slow probes, long login, and max task. Measure generated sample→accepted grant ages and accepted-grant gaps. Tests must not substitute the `ScheduleProof` constants for those measurements.

**F4 — P1: lease polling consumes the request cap, and reaching it silently disables renewal**

Evidence: `tart/machine/io.rs:278` increments `counts_lease` on every successful method return, including `Pending`; the adapter may have published nothing on that poll. With one lease request left pending and service every 100 ms, 2048 method calls use the machine's entire allowance in about 205 s. Meanwhile the adapter's `SeqTracker` correctly counts only newly issued requests (`tart/mailbox/seq.rs:115`). These are different counters.

After the machine counter reaches 2048, `should_renew` returns false (`tart/machine/service.rs:387`). Consequently `guest_lease`'s explicit cap error (`tart/machine/io.rs:262`) is not reached by normal automatic service. Ordinary side/inventory/probe work continues; an expired guest grant is merely cleared as described in F2. Exhaustion therefore does not reliably fail closed. There is also no frozen lease operation end across pending calls: each call is bounded afresh at `tart/machine/io.rs:267`.

Minimal fix: track issuance and polling separately. Freeze the lease's publication parameters and operation end; charge one request only when actually issued. When a required new sample/grant cannot be issued within either cap, enter the explicit failure/revoke path instead of suppressing service. Preserve old-ACK validation against the complete sent-grant table.

Regression: thousands of polls of one request must not consume thousands of request slots; request 2049 through ordinary machine service must fail closed without ordinary dispatch. Cover pending lease expiry without changing the caller's original end.

**F5 — P1: steps are not bounded to 100 ms, and expiry has no kill/reap transition**

Evidence: `tart/machine/io.rs:97` clips IO to a phase budget/aggregate, without a step-wide 100 ms end. First inventory, probe, and side calls receive their multi-second/minute operation end (`tart/machine/complete.rs:211`, `:229`, `tart/machine/service.rs:485`). Only follow-up polls add a 100 ms clip (`tart/machine/complete.rs:253`), separately for each call. A lease IO in `preamble` and a probe/inventory/side IO are executed within the same public step (`tart/machine/service.rs:147`, `:168`, `:182`), without a shared remaining slice budget.

Reserve/clone/start still perform multiple ledger/runner calls before returning (`tart/machine/steps.rs:85`, `:99`, `:109`, `:191`, `:203`, `:215`, `:265`, `:269`, `:273`). `destroy`/`reclaim` remain a synchronous list/status/stop/status/delete/list sequence (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:410`). These paths cannot service a four-second pending stop at 100 ms intervals. The trace called `stop_at_four_seconds_stays_inside_the_budget` advances the clock inside that single destroy call (`s11b/m4.rs:192`); it proves eventual owner suspension, not timely service during the call. The expiry trace explicitly permits one run call to consume **20 minutes** (`s11b/m4_guests.rs:203`, `s11b/m4.rs:276`).

On return, `late` only sets `dispatch_stopped` and moves to failure (`tart/machine/complete.rs:264`). Neither the runner interface nor this transition retains a pending command handle or requests/observes kill and reap. Declaring these responsibilities to belong to the outer owner in the commit message does not implement or test the required M4 lifecycle. Late owner expiry is also not universally rechecked before the next non-destructive dispatch: `issue` rechecks it only for destructive calls (`tart/machine/io.rs:149`).

Minimal fix: persist begin/poll progress for runner and guest operations, plus the necessary kill/reap state. At each public step establish one checked `step_start+100 ms` end and pass the remaining shared budget through **all** service, queue/lock, ledger, parsing, and IO work. Keep the long `op_end` separately. Return between slices of every slow operation. On in-slice expiry discard the response, prohibit ordinary dispatch, and drive bounded kill/reap; unresolved effects must quarantine.

Regression: charge fake time to each IO and to combinations such as renewal plus probe. Assert the whole public step is at most 100 ms, not just that an immediate fake leaves the clock unchanged. Hold stop pending for 4 s and observe service throughout; expire during IO and assert kill/reap ordering and unknown handling.

**F6 — P1: owner proofs and Stop are not serviced from reserve through seal**

Evidence: `ReserveInput` has one `owner_present` and one `stop_latched` boolean (`tart/machine/steps.rs:27`); these are checked only at reserve (`:65`, `:69`). There is no later Stop/owner-proof input to `preamble`, `gate`, or `issue`. Owner expiry is just an optional timestamp (`tart/machine/mod.rs:345`); `None` remains live indefinitely. The public function named `arm_owner_lease` calls **guest.lease** (`tart/machine/service.rs:256`) and sets `lease_until` from that guest ACK's clipped caller deadline (`:265`). It checks neither an external proof's attempt identity nor its increasing sequence or ≤1 s cadence.

An attempt that never calls this optional arm function can run through all preparation and task work without an expiring external owner proof. After revoke, the phase list in `arm_owner_lease` excludes `Revoking`/`Destroying` (`:235`), so the same API cannot maintain proofs throughout a legitimate 60 s cleanup. Existing cleanup tests only demonstrate eventual expiry of one manually armed 3 s timestamp, not continued external owner liveness. Guest ACKs must not establish host-owner authority.

Minimal fix: add an injected, offline-testable Stop/owner-proof service to the existing caller-owned machine. Validate attempt binding, increasing proof sequence, and the existing ≤1 s proof deadline from reserve through seal. Keep that state separate from guest grants and S3 scope. Service it before IO, result adoption, and destructive dispatch, including cleanup; expiry must remain permanently suspended. This does not require real provider wiring or a new thread.

Regression: deliver proofs during a long cleanup, stop proofs while guest ACKs continue, replay a proof, change its attempt, and latch Stop during pending IO. Verify ordinary dispatch stops at the first required service point and an expired owner never regains deletion/sealing authority.

**F7 — P1: nested completion rows are not correlated with dispatch, and observation is mistaken for consumption**

Evidence: `MailboxGuestChannel::absorb` accepts any shaped terminal active/revoke row (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:297`) without checking that its nested op/sequence was actually sent. It clears `effect` by op alone (`:300`) and queues consume fields immediately (`:306`, `:316`), before the machine's deadline, identity, outcome, and phase checks. `SeqTracker` validates only the outer inventory request, not its nested completion claims. Machine adoption checks a hardcoded nested sequence 1 (`tart/machine/complete.rs:33`, `:98`), not an issued/retained completion record. There is no machine consumption record: adoption clears the side fields (`:41`) while the adapter has already queued confirmation.

A valid inventory/1 envelope can report bootstrap/1 terminal **before bootstrap was sent**. Binding accepts it and the adapter holds/queues it. After the machine eventually dispatches bootstrap/1, another inventory carrying that same retained row passes `confirm_active` and is adopted as the current bootstrap's completion. The analogous unissued run/revoke rows are also accepted by the adapter. This violates the explicit future/unsent-sequence rule even though all outer envelope identity checks succeed.

Consumption is similarly premature for failed/unknown results, snapshots the machine rejects, or IO that crosses its deadline: `note_terminal`/`absorb` have already marked them for consumption (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:219`) before `read_inventory` calls `late` or `adopt`. The confirmation matcher does handle the exercised exact and empty/idempotent cases, but `gone` also accepts any different op as removal (`:244`) without first validating that replacement row against sent work. A bad replacement can thus be acknowledged and retained rather than treated as a protocol violation.

Minimal fix: maintain a private per-attempt issued/retained/consumed record for active and revoke rows. Validate nested op, request sequence, permitted transitions, and retained result identity before exposing a snapshot. Queue confirmation only after successful terminal handoff **and machine consumption recorded in that same transition**; preserve the public GuestChannel v1 signatures. Validate replacement rows before accepting a consume confirmation. Do not consume pending/accepted, rejected, late, or unknown completion as successful handoff.

Regression: inject unissued/future active and revoke rows on startup and during another op; observe a terminal before its dispatch and attempt to use it later; reject late/identity-invalid terminal snapshots without consumption. Cover exact repeat, consumed repeat, valid next active row, wrong replacement, and simultaneous active+revoke confirmation through the actual machine/adapter pair.

**F8 — P1: accepting revoke drops its original deadline from subsequent drain reads**

Evidence: first dispatch saves `revoke_end` (`tart/machine/service.rs:324`). Pending revoke-slot polls use it (`:350`). Once accepted, `revoke` goes directly to `drain_revoke` (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:69`), which calls `read_inventory(caller, Cleanup)` without clipping to `revoke_end` (`tart/machine/service.rs:369`). `owed_deadline` checks side/probe/inventory/group ends, but not revoke (`:291`); `inv_deadline` only adds the saved side end (`tart/machine/complete.rs:214`).

For example, revoke is accepted at t=0 with caller end t=2 s. If its first drain inventory is requested at t=3 s using a fresh caller deadline, it receives a new query deadline and can set `revoke_ok=true`. Repeated nonterminal drain inventories similarly continue beyond the 10 s revoke/drain cap, until the much longer cleanup/attempt aggregate stops them. A late success is therefore used as cleanup proof beyond the frozen operation end.

Minimal fix: include the saved revoke end in every drain read and adoption check, and in the old-deadline preamble. Expiry must be decided before new inventory/acceptance and must not be reset by an accepted reply or a fresh caller deadline. Preserve bounded stop/cleanup after drain failure.

Regression: accept revoke with a short caller deadline, delay the first drain past it, and return a valid terminal. Also cross the saved end during a pending drain and keep returning fresh nonterminal snapshots past 10 s. None may confirm revoke or extend its end.

**F9 — P2: machine cleanup compares against the stopped clone's pre-start boot**

Evidence: `start` invokes `confirm_owned` before runner start (`tart/machine/steps.rs:265`, `:273`); `confirm_owned` captures that status's boot into `session_boot` (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:458`). The first guest inventory's boot is discarded by normalization, and `observe_identity` synthesizes `GuestView.boot = "bound"` (`tart/machine/complete.rs:163`). Later cleanup statuses are compared only against the pre-start token (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:467`).

A stopped clone can report its previous boot A, then legitimately start as B and bind every mailbox envelope to B. Cleanup status B is then classified as `BootChanged`, discarding an otherwise valid score. Conversely, a missing pre-start status boot disables that independent status comparison entirely. The test status builder always supplies `boot1` (`s11b/harness.rs:305`), masking the provenance problem. The adapter's within-channel boot checks remain sound; this is the machine's separate status/evidence regression.

Minimal fix: retain the first verified inventory **envelope** boot in the private channel/machine binding context and use it as the authoritative session boot. Compare later guest envelopes exactly as today. If Tart's boot token is a separate identity domain, model and validate its relationship explicitly instead of substituting a pre-start token or a literal string. Keep GuestChannel v1 unchanged.

Regression: start from a stopped clone with old boot A, bind initial inventory to new boot B, and clean up B successfully. Then change a bound guest boot or the corresponding running status and require failure without adopting the replacement.

**F10 — P2: unknown side effects can finish with followups allowed**

Evidence: `adopt` clears the in-flight side state before examining its outcome (`tart/machine/complete.rs:41`). An unknown bootstrap/login/run result is reduced to ordinary `MissingProof` (`:62`, `:76`, `:84`). `fail` sets no unknown-effect/quarantine marker (`tart/machine/mod.rs:356`). If later revoke and VM cleanup succeed, seal considers cleanup confirmed (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:220`); `make_receipt` produces `NotRun(MissingProof)` (`:498`) and `followups_allowed=true` (`:518`).

Thus `run terminal/outcome=unknown`, followed by otherwise successful cleanup, loses the uncertainty and permits another attempt. That does not satisfy the requested unknown-effects quarantine path. F5's missing kill/reap/effect record makes this distinction particularly necessary; a generic failure reason does not establish whether the dispatched operation's effects are known.

Minimal fix: preserve a sticky unknown-effect classification separately from ordinary failed/missing evidence. Complete permitted cleanup, but retain quarantine/no-followups until the specified independent resolution/recovery proof exists. Do not queue successful consumption of an unknown effect (F7).

Regression: unknown results for all three ordinary side effects, lost results after dispatch, and unresolved kill/reap must retain unknown/quarantine and forbid followups, even if later VM absence is established.

**Existing test changes and gaps**

Most listed migrations preserve their original safety assertion: walking separate inventory terminals instead of immediate text success; looping the sharded probe group; expecting one extra early lease; refusing ordinary work after owner expiry; and checking prep/task/cleanup clipping. Moving successful revoke proof to a drain inventory is correct. These do not, however, validate timing or consumption merely by continuing to pass.

There are specific reductions in coverage:

- `foreign_ack_is_missing_proof` (`s11b/faults.rs:452`) formerly supplied a foreign run ID. It now supplies accepted bootstrap followed by a failed bootstrap outcome, with no foreign identity. Existing `reply_rejects_wrong_echo_and_binding` and `poll_guest_binding_mismatch_stays_failed` exercise nonce rejection (`tart/mailbox/tests/seq.rs:137`, `tart/mailbox/tests/slots.rs:722`), but do not replace the removed wrong-run machine/adapter case.

**Scope and properties that are present**

The diff stays offline: no real provider/Tart/admission wiring, dependency, enable feature, environment switch, or flag was added. `tart/guest.rs`, Cargo manifests, and the lockfile are unchanged; the seven methods and `GUEST_CHANNEL_VERSION=1` remain intact. No Stop constants were increased.

The source scan's change is the exact addition of `mailbox/tests/guest_sim.rs` to the known test-source list (`crates/gcu-eval/tests/tart_offline.rs:975`), with `#![cfg(test)]` at that source's first line. The classifier still compares platform path components (`:1074`); unsafe remains exempt only in `mailbox/sys.rs`. An independent in-memory spelling/gating check over all 38 Tart Rust sources found no forbidden spellings outside the existing exact exemptions and no missing first-line test gate in the six exempt test sources. This is a source-boundary check, not a Rust execution or OS sandbox proof.

Repeated polls do not republish an open request (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:177`); actual mailbox errors become sticky. Ordinary side dispatch checks one `effect` at a time (`:322`), inventory remains independent, and revoke can be sent while an ordinary op is pending. Direct bootstrap/login/run/revoke slot terminals are rejected by the M3 layer and by the machine's new transitions. Pending/accepted bodies do not directly produce ACK/login/score/drain proof. The incomplete validation/consumption path is F7.

Task aggregate begins at first run (`tart/machine/service.rs:214`, `tart/machine/io.rs:125`); side operation ends and probe/group ends are saved instead of refreshed on each inventory. Prep includes login, and the attempt hard deadline remains part of normal IO clipping. Checked additions protect the host instant bounds and `g+2000` (`tart/mailbox/seq.rs:363`, `tart/machine/service.rs:547`); the missing properties are sample age, grant continuity, universal service timing, lease/revoke saved-end handling, and effect resolution identified above.

The commit message accurately discloses several omissions, but those omissions are required M4 behavior under ADR §3/§6. Passing the existing fake traces does not close them. The implementation should remain unwired pending these fixes and composed offline regressions.
