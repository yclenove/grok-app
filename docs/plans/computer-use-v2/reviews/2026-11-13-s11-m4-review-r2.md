**R2-1 — P1: the schedule still proves constants that the machine/adapter cannot maintain**

Evidence: `tart/schedule.rs:26` fixes visibility at 1000 ms, `:40` derives age 1100 ms, and `:82` accepts the window without any timing input. `tart/machine/service.rs:619` uses that nominal proof. Grant publication is now correctly independent of ACK arrival (`:372`, `:520`, `:565`), but inventory still cannot publish while its previous request is open (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:189`). Binding returns immediately after reading its first terminal (`tart/machine/service.rs:181`); the next step services the lease before starting the next inventory (`:177`, `:186`). Subsequent completion sampling has the same one-open-inventory restriction (`:720`).

A conforming counterexample within the advertised 1 s round trip is: inventory 1 published at host t=0, guest generates its sample at t=900 ms, host reads it at t=1000 ms; the next public step at t=1100 publishes lease 1 and inventory 2. Lease 1 reaches the guest at t=2000, sample age 1100 ms. Inventory 2's sample reaches the host at t=2100. However, `note_sample` installed a host end of **t=2000** from the first inventory receipt (`tart/machine/complete.rs:299`), and `preamble` permanently stops work at that end (`tart/machine/service.rs:356`). Publishing another lease at t=1900 only reuses sample 1 and cannot move that end. The attempt fails before its second fresh sample, despite satisfying the claimed visibility and service figures. An 800 ms publication timer does not establish an 800 ms fresh-sample or accepted-grant grid.

The 1000 ms constant is also a typical value, not the measured upper bound. ADR 0019:327 reports observed round-trip maxima **1023/1021 ms**, from eight samples per direction, and `:343` requires margin under load. Substituting 1023 gives `2000−1023−100−800=77 ms`, not 100 ms. Even that observed maximum is not an established worst-case bound. `due(last_publication, now)` allows up to another service interval before a due publication (`tart/schedule.rs:115`); no demonstrated correlation removes that jitter from the gap. With a further 100 ms gap delay the arithmetic is −23 ms. Variable visibility also makes accepted-grant gaps differ from publication gaps. Nothing measures or rejects those conditions in `prove`.

There is a related validity gap: every distinct inventory sample advances `grant_until` before any corresponding grant is accepted (`tart/machine/complete.rs:285`), while pending leases permit ordinary work. There is no separate accepted-grant horizon. A channel that keeps producing inventories while lease acceptance stops can advance host validity beyond the guest's last accepted grant; a sample is not proof that an expired guest grant remained continuous. The explicit same-sample/expired-host-end guards are useful, but do not establish this missing continuity.

The count arithmetic **is correct conditionally**: `1_560_000/800+1+8=1959`, leaving 89 requests per stream. It does not prove that the actual protocol can run on that grid. Minimal fix: model the actual inventory request/sample/visibility cycle and accepted grant continuity, include measured direction/service/gap bounds, and fail the feasibility gate when they do not fit. Keep candidate sample bounds separate from the grant known to cover work. Do not increase lease/Stop limits. Regression: compose `Attempt`, the real mailbox adapter, and a clock-driven guest for the full window, including this asymmetric 900+100 ms trace, the observed 1023 ms case, jitter, ENOENT, skipped lease versions, and lost ACKs; measure guest acceptance ages/gaps and termination.

**R2-2 — P1: a fresh owner proof can replace an already-expired proof before expiry is checked**

Evidence: `open_step` calls `service_owner` first (`tart/machine/mod.rs:457`). `service_owner_until` polls the new proof without checking the stored old deadline (`:328`), and `accept_owner` overwrites `lease_until` (`:364`). The later `ensure_phase`/`issue` expiry checks therefore see the replacement (`:500`; `tart/machine/io.rs:175`). `seal` and `observe_owner_lease` have the same ordering (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:198`, `:336`).

For example, accept owner proof n ending at t=1000 ms, enter cleanup, and next call `destroy` at t=1500 after installing proof n+1 ending at t=2500. The new proof passes binding, sequence, and ≤1 s lifetime validation, and cleanup can stop/delete/seal; the expired old proof never produces `Suspended`. The default live fake also demonstrates this path after a scheduling gap because its next poll mints a new proof (`tart/owner.rs:112`). Already-observed suspension remains closed, but an unobserved expiry can be revived.

Minimal fix: check and permanently apply the old owner deadline before polling or accepting any replacement proof, at every entry and result/dispatch service point. Regression: install a strictly newer, otherwise-valid proof after the old end, including before destroy and seal; require permanent suspension and no deletion/seal. Also cover owner expiry while kill/reap is pending.

**R2-3 — P1: Stop/owner service is missing inside steps, and latched Stop prevents its own cleanup**

Evidence: `issue` checks only the stored owner timestamp, not the injected inbox/Stop latch (`tart/machine/io.rs:165`); `_destructive` is ignored. Successful IO bodies are returned without polling owner/Stop (`:226`), ledger results have no post-call validation (`:453`), and completion adoption immediately records/uses evidence (`tart/machine/complete.rs:49`, `:104`, `:130`). Thus Stop latched inside a stop/status call can be followed by another status and **delete in the same public step** (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:502`, `:515`, `:534`). Stop latched while reading a terminal can likewise be followed by consumption and adoption. The controller explicitly requires service before result adoption and destructive dispatch, not only at public entry.

Once the inbox does report Stop, `service_owner_until` sets `halted`, calls `fail`, and returns `StopLatched` (`tart/machine/mod.rs:330`). It does not arm priority revoke/kill. Every subsequent `revoke`/`destroy` entry polls the same permanently latched Stop and returns before cleanup (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:49`, `:132`). This strands the machine in `Revoking`; cleanup cannot make progress through its public APIs. `stop_during_pending_cleanup_does_not_delete` actually asserts that **no runner stop occurs** (`s11b/m4.rs:1174`), rather than checking bounded Stop cleanup.

Minimal fix: make owner/Stop service a common pre-dispatch and post-IO/pre-adoption gate; retain the Stop latch while allowing a bounded priority revoke/release/kill/reap cleanup state to progress. Regression: latch Stop from inside IO and ledger fakes, including immediately before result handoff and between stop/status/delete; verify no adoption or unauthorized delete, and observe timely cleanup with Stop still latched.

**R2-4 — P1: the shared 100 ms end is dropped after exhaustion, and several slow operations still have no resumable cursor**

Evidence: the new `step_end` is created once (`tart/machine/mod.rs:302`), but `admit` passes the **long operation end** to the lock (`:533`, `:541`). Both `bound` and `follow` apply the step clip only while `now < step_end`; after the step is exhausted they deliberately remove that bound (`tart/machine/io.rs:132`; `tart/machine/complete.rs:340`). Reserve/clone/start/seal still chain lock, ledger, runner, parsing, and writes without a cursor/yield between each stage (`tart/machine/steps.rs:89`, `:103`, `:113`, `:198`, `:210`, `:222`, `:235`, `:275`; `tart/machine/cleanup.rs:249`, `:260`). `ledger_at` returns a successful late result directly (`tart/machine/io.rs:453`); parsing and `note_consumed` have no shared-end checks.

There is an existing executable counterexample in the supplied passing suite: `prep_end_must_include_reserve` advances the lock by **500 ms**, then requires `reserve_default` to succeed (`s11b/review_fix.rs:693`). The lock receives the long end, consumes the half second, and later `bound` calls skip the expired 100 ms clip. This disproves the universal whole-step claim without inventing an uncooperative implementation.

The new fake cost model also does not slice slow clone/start: it refuses any call whose complete cost does not fit (`tart/runner.rs:176`; `tart/guest.rs:172`). Clone has already consumed its permit before that refusal (`tart/machine/steps.rs:218`), and does not retain progress; start failures similarly return without a begin/poll command handle. These paths cannot demonstrate completion of an operation longer than a slice. The 40+40 ms guest test only covers calls that already fit (`s11b/m4.rs:934`). The real mailbox adapter checks its deadline once at entry (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:91`); publication, recursive read retries, and parsing receive no end and can occupy the entire caller thread past it (`:138`, `:173`; `tart/mailbox/slots.rs:586`).

Minimal fix: keep the shared end binding even when due, pass it to lock/ledger/parse/IO, and yield before further work when exhausted. Preserve long ends separately and persist begin-once/poll state for reserve, clone, start, seal, and slow runner/FS operations. Check deadlines and owner/Stop again before adopting results. Regression: charge lock/ledger/parse and IO costs, retain the existing 500 ms lock case as a refusal/yield case, and finish slow commands through multiple ≤100 ms calls without redispatch or losing permits.

**R2-5 — P1: lost/error results still bypass unknown-effect quarantine and kill/reap**

Evidence: an explicit `Outcome::Unknown` now sets `effect_unknown` and arms kill (`tart/machine/complete.rs:40`), and the final receipt correctly forbids followups when that flag is set (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:629`, `:659`). However, `phase_out` handles an error after a side dispatch by only calling `fail` (`tart/machine/io.rs:222`). The kill/unknown transition exists only for a **successful** body returned late (`:226`). An `Err(CallError::Expired)` at the deadline, `TimedOut`, lost reply, malformed completion, or channel failure after acceptance can leave a dispatched side effect unresolved without setting the flag. `note_loss` also only calls `fail` (`tart/machine/mod.rs:620`).

Counterexample: run is dispatched and its call/result is lost or times out; before the saved long side end, valid revoke/drain and VM cleanup complete. Nothing in the generic failure path marks uncertainty. `seal` sees `revoke_ok && vm_cleanup_ok && !halted && !effect_unknown` (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:242`) and can return `NotRun(Timeout/ReplyLost)` with followups allowed. No kill/reap need occur. This is the original F10 uncertainty-loss bug for transport/lifecycle failures, although explicit unknown terminals are fixed.

Minimal fix: distinguish proved pre-dispatch refusal from unknown post-dispatch failure, retain the issued effect until a definitive result is accepted, and set sticky uncertainty/kill-reap for lost or expired results. Successful later VM cleanup must not clear that classification. Regression: each ordinary side op with lost result, error exactly at the slice end, failed completion read, and provider/guest loss after acceptance; require quarantine/no followups after otherwise successful cleanup.

**R2-6 — P1: kill/reap has no frozen operation deadline and bypasses owner/Stop service**

Evidence: `begin_public` drives `reap_slice` **before** owner/Stop service or closed-phase checks (`tart/machine/mod.rs:308`, `:457`). `reap_slice` calls the runner directly with a new step end every time (`:421`, `:425`, `:436`), without the attempt/cleanup/cancellation end or any saved kill/reap end. `Expired` while still before that fresh end is treated as an indefinite retry (`:430`, `:445`). Any successful kill body advances the state, and any reap body without the substring `unknown` resolves it (`:429`, `:440`), with no post-call lateness or exact exit-proof validation.

With `set_io_cost(101 ms)`, the supplied fake refuses every 100 ms kill slice without moving time or consuming work. Repeated public calls remain in `Reap::Kill` forever, even as the caller advances time beyond owner, attempt, and termination budgets. Owner/Stop is never polled along that path. The positive tests assert only that a Kill and Reap command exist (`s11b/m4.rs:344`, `:1339`); `set_reap_unknown` has no test caller.

Minimal fix: retain command handles and one checked cancellation/kill/reap end, service owner/Stop every slice, accept only timely exact exit proof, and terminate unresolved cancellation as sticky unknown/quarantine within the required bound. Regression: pending kill/reap across many slices, deadline exhaustion, late/malformed/pending reap bodies, explicit unknown, and owner/Stop expiry during those slices; assert ordering and elapsed bounds, not just command presence.

**R2-7 — P1: the new recovery cursor leaks between rows and can bypass process-exit proof**

Evidence: `reclaim_at` is one machine-wide cursor (`tart/machine/mod.rs:226`). Successful reclaim leaves it at 5 (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:539`) and normal destroy explicitly resets it (`:172`), but recovery does not reset or bind it to a row (`tart/machine/recover.rs:67`, `:94`, `:102`). At cursor 5, reclaim skips list-presence/start checks, ownership/status, stop, stopped/exited proof, and delete, going straight to the final list (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:466`, `:483`, `:544`).

Recover two allocated rows: A completes normal reclamation, leaving cursor 5; B has `StartIssued` and an expected identity, but its name is missing from the authoritative list while process exit remains unproved. B now skips the cursor-0 rule that explicitly rejects this case (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:471`), treats a missing name as cleanup success, seals the row, and emits cleanup-confirmed/followups-allowed (`tart/machine/recover.rs:115`, `:132`). A pause/resume or reusing the recovered machine for a new attempt can also reuse the stale cursor. This is a regression introduced by the fix.

Minimal fix: bind all reclamation progress and saved ends to the current row identity, preserve that identity across yields, and reset the cursor on row completion and before new attempts. Regression: two rows, including a started-but-list-absent second row, and pauses at every reclaim stage; require independent ownership and process-exit proof for each row.

**R2-8 — P1: a drain that reuses pre-revoke pending inventory still escapes the saved revoke end**

Evidence: the new revoke clip is applied only when starting a **fresh** inventory (`tart/machine/complete.rs:272`). The `inv_pending` branch returns earlier through `follow(inv_end, caller)` (`:258`), preserving an end established before revoke. `drain_revoke` checks the revoke end before starting IO (`tart/machine/service.rs:491`), but `adopt_revoke` never checks it after that IO (`tart/machine/complete.rs:114`). Its `late` check only sees the inventory/slice end (`:168`).

Counterexample: an ordinary completion inventory is pending with a long query end; cancel moves the attempt into cleanup, then revoke is accepted with an end 50 ms away. Begin drain at +20 ms with a fresh longer caller deadline, and return a valid revoke terminal after 40 ms of IO. The old pending inventory is clipped to the step end at +120 ms, rather than the revoke end at +50 ms. At +60 ms the body passes the inventory/slice checks and sets `revoke_ok=true`, although the original revoke end has passed. Both supplied saved-end cases create their pending inventory after revoke and therefore miss this branch (`s11b/m4.rs:1191`, `:1238`).

Minimal fix: intersect **every** inventory poll with the saved revoke end when draining, including pre-existing pending reads, and recheck that end immediately before accepting/consuming revoke proof. Regression: leave an ordinary inventory pending, enter cleanup, accept a short revoke, and cross its end during the reused inventory read; no revoke confirmation or consumption may occur.

**R2-9 — P2: consumption is queued before the machine decides that the terminal is acceptable**

Evidence: `adopt` calls `note_consumed` based only on `Outcome::Ok` (`tart/machine/complete.rs:49`), before the op-specific acceptance and evidence transition (`:65`). `LoginState::Missing` is a legal value with outcome `ok` (`tart/mailbox/ops.rs:139`), so login/missing is recorded as consumed and queued by the adapter (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:512`), and then rejected by `adopt_login` (`tart/machine/complete.rs:90`). A later cleanup inventory publishes that consume despite the machine's `MissingProof` decision. There is no machine record made after acceptance that authorizes the earlier queueing.

Minimal fix: validate identity, deadline, result kind/value, and phase first; commit the evidence/consumption record in the accepted transition, then queue the exact consume in that same step. Regression: the actual machine/adapter pair must reject login/missing, late/identity-invalid results, failed and unknown outcomes without publishing consumption, while successful active/revoke adoption does publish it. The adapter-only manual calls to `note_consumed` do not establish this ordering.

**R2-10 — P2: repeated sample timestamps desynchronize the machine's saved grant from the adapter's actual request**

Evidence: the adapter updates `sample_seq` on every inventory terminal (`tart/mailbox_guest.rs:227`) and uses that sequence in the next lease (`:469`). The machine returns early for the same `sample_ms` before updating `sample_inv` (`tart/machine/complete.rs:289`). `issue_grant` then saves the old inventory request sequence (`tart/machine/service.rs:551`), while `lease_matches` requires that stale sequence in the ACK (`:756`).

Two legal inventory samples with the same monotonic millisecond but different request sequences must not extend the deadline; they are still distinct sampled records. After such a pair, a correct ACK echoing the adapter's newer inventory reference is rejected as malformed by the machine. Fast off-grid completion samples can encounter this; the immediate fakes' constant inventory sequence conceals it.

Minimal fix: update the sample reference without extending its fixed validity, or explicitly pin the exact reference used by both layers when publishing. Regression: same timestamp in inventory n and n+1, then a real adapter lease and ACK; require exact saved-request agreement and an unchanged host end.

**R2-11 — P2: inventory slice refusals are still charged as issued requests**

Evidence: `read_inventory` calls `bump_inv` before calling the channel (`tart/machine/complete.rs:160`); `bump_inv` increments the count and moves the publication timestamp immediately (`:243`). If the shared slice cannot fit the fake IO cost, `phase_out` marks a pause (`tart/machine/io.rs:218`) and the caller suppresses it, but no inventory was issued and neither count nor timestamp is restored. `inv_pending` remains false, so the next attempt charges another request. For example, a first binding inventory with a configured 101 ms fake cost never issues in its 100 ms slice, yet repeated calls increment the counter up to the cap while time stays unchanged. A 60 ms pending lease followed by a 60 ms inventory in the same step also charges a refused inventory against the remaining 40 ms. This violates the controller's actual-issued-only rule and can prematurely reach `MAX_POLLS`.

Minimal fix: obtain issuance status from the channel's private accounting and charge/update publication time only on actual publication; polling and pre-dispatch slice refusal must not count. Regression: thousands of normal machine steps with a refused first inventory, followed by successful publication; then exhaust request 2049 through ordinary service and require fail-closed dispatch. The adapter's normal cap test is useful but does not cover this machine counter.

**R2-12 — P2: the separate Tart boot domain is still compared directly with the guest envelope token**

Evidence: session boot now correctly comes from `GuestAccounting::envelope_boot` (`tart/machine/complete.rs:195`; `tart/mailbox_guest.rs:489`), and pre-start status no longer supplies it. But the first cleanup Tart status compares its token directly with `guest_boot` and sets `BootChanged` on inequality (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:589`). The controller explicitly chose separate identity domains. Different tokens can describe the same valid session; the correct pair is not required to have equal spellings.

Thus guest boot `guest-B` with stable Tart boot `tart-B` completes deletion but receives a spurious `NotRun(BootChanged)` and loses its provisional score (`tart/machine/cleanup.rs:639`). `tart_boot_follows_envelope_not_pre_start` supplies equal `bootB` tokens and only checks reaching `Destroying` in its positive case (`s11b/m4.rs:1256`), so it misses both the domain error and final-score loss. A Tart change before its first cleanup observation is also not distinguished from an initially different domain token.

Minimal fix: record an explicitly associated guest/Tart identity pair after start/binding, compare subsequent values within their own domains, and validate the association without string equality. Regression: old pre-start A, guest B, independently named Tart T, successful scored seal; then independently change each bound domain and reject the replacement.

**R2-13 — P2: one bounded read can exceed 128 KiB, and retry bytes disappear from accounting**

Evidence: `read_body` charges bytes only when a read was replaced and checks the total only **after** that read (`tart/mailbox/slots.rs:641`, `:645`). A stable final read never checks or adds `charged` (`:654`). Consequently 32 replaced 4096-byte reads consume exactly 131072 bytes, recurse, and then accept any nonempty valid stable frame beyond the cap. If the final read is replaced too, rejection occurs only after exceeding the cap. `read_slot` discards the accumulated byte total (`:343`), reporting zero on failure (`:345`); successful `poll_guest` reports only the final frame length (`:419`). Retry counters survive, but byte evidence understates actual work.

Minimal fix: budget the next read from remaining bytes, charge every read including the final stable one, carry cumulative bytes through success/failure, and use an iterative bounded retry path with the shared time end. Regression: exact 128 KiB boundary followed by a stable legal frame, and a final replaced frame; assert no read beyond the allowance and accurate success/failure byte totals. The new `poll_byte_budget_fails_closed` only checks endless replaced 4000-byte reads and sticky failure (`tart/mailbox/tests/guest_sim.rs:292`).
