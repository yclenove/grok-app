# S11-M4b independent design review, round 3 — 2026-11-15

**Verdict: request changes. One P1 feasibility finding; no P0 or additional P2 findings.** The event-driven grant obligation removes the independent lease-cadence defect, and the inventory floor closes the fast-visibility count problem. D2-r2 is not completely closed: the revised scheduler's simultaneous publication obligations do not always fit its shared step, and its timing bounds omit that contention.

Reviewed branch `cu2/s11-m4b-design`, HEAD `0b9936f2c4fa4cb62f8e559f572aa818c627a443`, the round-1 and round-2 reports, relevant ADR 0019 requirements, and current publication/adapter/schedule sources. `D:n` denotes line n of `docs/plans/computer-use-v2/s11-m4b-design.md` at this HEAD. Scope is limited to the three requested round-3 checks. Only this report was written; no commit, build, repository test, VM, mount, network operation or real-channel experiment was performed. Arithmetic and the trace below were checked in memory, not through an implemented machine regression.

**D2-r3 — P1: a fresh grant and the next inventory can both be mandatory in a step that cannot publish both.**

Evidence: D:143 requires the next inventory in the service step selected by `max(next step after acceptance, h_pre+800)`. D:145 requires a fresh pin's grant in the acceptance step if it fits, otherwise within the next step. D:164–165 orders the grant before inventory, shares the same 100 ms budget and requires the next-step obligation to be honored. D:47 makes each publication indivisible across polls. D:178 explicitly requires the 60 ms lease plus 60 ms inventory cost case; D:169 also charges positive acceptance, parsing and service costs. Nevertheless, D:150–152 and D:207 require `D≤2246`, `A≤2146`, next-grant visibility by `g+4392`, and host replacement within 4492 ms.

Consider this phase on the required 100 ms service grid, with both visibility legs exactly 1023 ms. Publications take 60 ms, with rename 1 ms after their start and the remainder spent completing the publication. The host inventory poll/parse/accept path takes 60 ms; leading owner/service work takes 1 ms. These are bounded cooperative costs, with no loss, overdue owner proof, unknown publication or exhausted count.

| Event | Host time, ms |
| --- | ---: |
| Inventory 1 `h_pre` / rename / successful completion | 0 / 1 / 60 |
| Guest sample `g_1` / reply visible to host | 1024 / 2047 |
| Host service step / leading service / pin accepted | 2100 / 2101 / 2161 |
| Remaining acceptance-step budget | 39 |
| Required grant: `h_pre` / rename / completion in next step | 2201 / 2202 / 2261 |
| Remaining budget in that step | 39 |
| Grant becomes guest-visible | 3225 |
| Earliest following-step inventory: `h_pre` / sample `g_2` | 2301 / 3325 |

The new pin cannot receive its 60 ms grant in the 39 ms left after acceptance. In the next step, the grant and the next inventory are both due: the old inventory floor was already reached at t=800. Their two 60 ms publications cannot fit in 100 ms even before service costs. Publishing both violates the shared deadline; deferring the grant violates its priority obligation; deferring inventory violates D:143. Starting a publication and carrying its remaining 21 ms into another poll is expressly excluded by D:47. Failing closed here does not supply the full-window feasibility witness required by D:193 and D:206–207.

Even allowing the sensible inventory deferral, this trace has sample-to-grant age `3225−1024=2201>2146` and sample gap `3325−1024=2301>2246`. Thus the numerical bounds are not derived consequences of the scheduler with the required positive costs. This example does **not** demonstrate guest expiry under the new scheduler: the fixed 5 s window still has room. It demonstrates an infeasible pair of mandatory dispatch times and unattainable universal regression assertions. The 100 ms allowance cannot simultaneously represent reply pickup/acceptance, a deferred grant publication, and the conflicting next inventory publication.

**Minimal design change:** retain the next-step priority grant and one-grant-per-pin rule. Permit the next inventory to use the first budget-fitting service step after that grant, explicitly bound this deferral, and charge reply acceptance, publication and service phase offsets in the joint proof. Recompute the guest and host bounds from that executable schedule, preserving the fixed 5000 ms expiry and ACK policy. If a required individual publication plus service cannot fit one step, reject that cost contract rather than attempting a partial publication. Add the table's acceptance-tail/60+60 collision to the composed success regressions; small positive costs alone can hide it.

**Timing and count re-derivation.** Conditional on the asserted bounds actually holding, the continuity arithmetic is sufficient: `C=D=2×1023+2×100=2246`, `A=2×1023+100=2146`, `D+A=4392<5000`, and `D+C=4492<5000`. For each delivered replacement grant, `accept_(k+1)≤g_(k+1)+A≤g_k+D+A<g_k+5000`; induction prevents expiry after the first timely grant. Separately, timely acceptance of the replacement pin before `h_pre_k+D+C` preserves the host gate. No ACK return leg participates. The finding above prevents certifying those premises for every allowed positive-cost phase, so the requested universal in-model no-expiry conclusion is not yet established by this design.

The count bound does hold independently of latency. D:143–144 applies `h_pre_(k+1)−h_pre_k≥800` to every inventory, including completion/consume sampling. In a window of length W this permits at most `floor(W/800)+1` publication positions. Adding the separately reserved eight samples is conservative: `1,560,000/800+1+8=1959`, leaving 89 below 2048. One unique lease per fresh pin, with no second lease for equal g, gives `N_lease≤N_inventory≤1959` (D:146, D:154–155). No one-way latency lower bound or slow-path cycle is needed. Deferring inventory to resolve the finding cannot increase this count.

**Other scheduler interactions.** No separate blocker was found in the specified one-open-inventory rule, ACK handling or cleanup ordering. D:123–124 and D:144–146 preserve completed-publication accounting and sticky unknown without retrying an uncertain grant. D:134 allows the first safe grant during identity pairing; D:159 and D:209 allow work before a current ACK without treating an ACK as authority. D:84–87 and D:164 put Stop/revoke/release ahead of grants; D:145 ends renewal at revoke, and D:112–116 retains the original drain end and independent stop path. The inventory floor limits newly created grant obligations, so grant priority alone cannot create an unbounded stream that starves ordinary work. Service rotation in D:165 remains necessary for pending inventory/ACK polling; resolving the publication collision must preserve it.

An unconditional assertion that the guest can never expire while the host gate is open would contradict the deliberate lease-delivery-loss negative control in D:211. For that out-of-model case, the required property is irreversible guest stop and no post-expiry effect, not host knowledge of grant acceptance. This review does not request an ACK work gate.

**Regression adequacy for the requested historical cases.** D:194–205 reproduces the round-2 counterexample, including positive publication cost, due-time phase slip and strict expiry at 14824 ms while the host end remains 16000 ms. D:206–207 then requires the new scheduler, actual adapter and autonomous guest to sustain both named latency cases through every tested phase for the full window. That would catch the original independent-cadence defect. D:208 separately exercises 1+1 ms visibility, strict fresh samples, positive costs, the 100 ms grid, actual `h_pre` spacing and successful publication counts; removing the 800 ms floor would fail it even if the slow-path tests passed. Those cases are appropriately specified, but they do not repair the incompatible cost/timing obligations identified above.
